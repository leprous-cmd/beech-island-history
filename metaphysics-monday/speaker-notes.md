# Metaphysics Monday: Are You Still You?
## Speaker Notes — High School Philosophy

### Slide 1 — Title
Say something like: "Today we're asking a question that sounds simple but isn't: are you still you?" Pause. Let it land. Don't answer it yet.

### Slide 2 — Warm-Up Prompt
Give them two minutes, silent writing. Don't collect the lists — this is private inventory. The point is that they each have an intuitive list before you complicate it.

### Slide 3 — The Surgeon's Question
This is where it gets uncomfortable. Most students will say "somewhere in the middle." Press them: is it cell number four million? Cell number four million and one? If there's no line, what does that say about the idea of a "you" made of parts?

### Slide 4 — The Gradual Swap
Walk through it slowly. One cell a day. Don't rush to the year-seven punchline — let them do the math in their heads. Then hit it: zero original cells. Ask the room directly: dead or continuing? Take a vote. You'll get both answers, and that's fine.

### Slide 5 — Same Stuff vs. Same Story
Introduce the Ship of Theseus here if it hasn't come up. The key distinction: a photocopy has the same *content* but isn't the same *object*. A story retold has the same *meaning* but different *words*. Which one is a person more like?

### Slide 6 — The Hard One
This is the slide where the room goes quiet, then loud. Read it slowly. Emphasize: the copy believes it's you. It has your memories, your plans, your relationships from the inside. The original is still there, unchanged. Don't resolve it. Just ask: which one is you?

### Slide 7 — The Loud Question
Follow up immediately with the practical stakes: house, job, relationships. This forces them past the abstract into something they can feel. Someone will say "the original, obviously." Ask why. Someone else will say "the copy, because it thinks it's me." Ask why. Don't pick a side.

### Slide 8 — Closing Thought
Deliver this as the last word, not the first. The wave metaphor reframes everything: maybe identity was never about the stuff at all. It's about the doing. You can leave them with this and walk out — or open the floor for one final round.

### Slide 9 — Optional: Go Deeper
Only use if you have time or a sharp group. Parfit's teleporter is the cleanest extension. Fission is the hardest. The neuroscience hook grounds it in something real if the class is drifting into pure abstraction.

### Slide 10 — Norms
Say this at the start if your class needs it, or at the end as a recap. The "say why before you say what" rule is the single most useful habit you can leave them with — it transfers to every subject.
