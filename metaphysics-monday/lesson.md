# Metaphysics Monday: Are You Still You?
## High School Philosophy — Personal Identity Lesson

### Learning Goal
Students explore whether personal identity depends on physical continuity or on pattern/narrative, and confront the limits of "same stuff" versus "same story."

---

### Warm-Up (10 min)
- Have students list five things that make them *them*: a scar, a memory, a habit, a relationship, a belief.
- Ask: if a surgeon replaced each one, one at a time, at what point did you stop being you?
- Most will answer "somewhere in the middle" — which is already a problem, because there's no clean line.

### Main Activity: The Gradual Swap (20 min)
- Thought experiment: replace one cell per day. By year seven, zero original cells remain.
- Key questions:
  - Did you die at some point and get replaced by a copy?
  - Or did you just... continue?
- Push the distinction between "same stuff" and "same story."
- Connect to the Ship of Theseus: a ship whose planks are replaced one by one — is it still the same ship?

### Harder Ponder (15 min)
- A machine scans your brain perfectly and builds an identical copy that wakes up believing it's you — your memories, your plans. The original you is still standing there.
- Which one gets your life? Your house, your job, your relationships?
- Let the room get loud. That's the point.

### Closing Line
- Maybe a person isn't a thing at all. Maybe it's an event — like a wave isn't the water, it's what the water is doing.

### Optional Extensions
- Derek Parfit's "teleporter" variant: you step in, you step out on Mars. Did you travel, or did you die?
- The "fission" case: one person splits into two copies. Which is the original?
- Real-world hook: memory, personality, and the brain — what neuroscience says about identity.

### Discussion Norms
- No wrong answers, only undefended ones.
- Require students to say *why* before they say *what*.
